I was born in 2001, and that makes me 23 years old right now. I don't remember the exact video or even the exact year I watched it in when I started watching youtube. My best guess in placing my earliest memories using youtube on a timeline is in the third grade (around 2009) but I'm sure I probably first encountered the site earlier. [Some of you reading this remember how it was on the site back in 2012, when my memories start getting clearer. Back then, Youtube had more features that encouraged interaction between users]

I know my case isn't unique. Many other people from my generation have grown up with youtube as their main source of entertainment. What sets me apart from all of them is the sheer amount of time that place stole from me. 

What did 16 years of habitually watching youtube do to me? Unfortunately, I have no idea. [But what I can say is that it hasn't just influenced my beliefs, but ] Some people can look back on experiences like drug addiction or unhealthy eating habits and say definitively that they regret it. But for me to say that I regret all of the time I spent on youtube would be like tossing half of my personality in the trash. "Half" may even be an understatement. My taste in what I find pleasant about a person, in art and design; social skills, political beliefs, how I present myself, the media I love, the grades I got in school, and how I learn all change if I reject youtube.

The way someone engages with videos probably has some effect on the quality of time they have on the platform. In my case, I rarely ever watch videos in the background. It's always the only thing in front of me, or I'm not on the site at all. This is actually a part of the problem I have with youtube. Long videos have been around for a while, but it's becoming more common for creators to post deep-dive "essays" that run over the hour-long mark. As I watch one of these videos, I *feel* like I'm learning everything, but I click away left with nothing. Whatever knowledge I gain from the video is ultimately useless and uninteresting to me, and I'm inundated with so much of it from every video that it disappears from my mind in a few hours. I've seen people who think the main problem with the way they engage with youtube is that they aren't focused on it enough. Maybe there's some truth to this. There are creators on youtube that make well-researched, high-quality video essays on important topics, and you might even have some specific ones in mind right now. But think: do you remember even a single point from one of these videos right now? Could you put its general idea into words? For me, the answer is no.

I used to think of watching videos as no different from reading books. Of course, the attention you have to pay towards reading text requires more brain power than just letting the sounds and pictures wash past you when you "watch" a video. But the way I saw it, this phenomenon of consuming media without thinking about it can also happen as you read. Sometimes I find myself having finished passing my eyes over a paragraph and realize I have no idea what I just read.

The difference between print and video lies in subtleties. If I don't know what I've just read, I can just read the last few sentences again. There is no physical actions associated with this. What is the equivalent of this for a youtube video? I have to get up from whatever position I'm comfortable in, reach towards my mouse and scrub back to the point I last remember, which I might have some trouble finding. If I'm watching on my tablet or phone, I can double tap the screen to go back 10 seconds, but this rarely puts me at the time I'm looking for. Most of the time, I don't care enough to rewatch a portion of the video. 

Does this mean youtube would be healthier if there were some magical browser extension that made navigating videos as easy as navigating your eyes on a page? Do I need one of those eye tracking mice so I don't need to move? Am I just too lazy to watch youtube videos? Maybe. But over the years, I've realized that video is simply inferior to print in keeping my full attention and conveying information. It's also a huge waste of time. The mechanism with which youtube keeps me "engaged" on their site is through the endless offering of recommendations and the simple fact that I've been on there for 16 years, not the content of the videos anymore.
